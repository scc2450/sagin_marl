\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{S}{pace-air-ground} integrated networks can extend service
coverage by coordinating terrestrial users, aerial relays, and satellite
links. This manuscript draft will frame the problem as dynamic task scheduling
under queueing pressure, UAV mobility, satellite visibility, access bandwidth,
and safety constraints.

The introduction should eventually answer three questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Why is SAGIN/UAV-satellite scheduling hard under dynamic queues and visibility?
    \item Why do standard rule-based or optimization-style schedulers leave a gap?
    \item What structured learning contribution does this paper make?
\end{itemize}

The planned contribution structure is:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a topology-aware SAGIN scheduling formulation;
    \item a structured multi-agent learning scheduler with staged movement, satellite, and bandwidth decisions;
    \item a reproducible evaluation against strong heuristic and Lyapunov/MaxWeight baselines;
    \item zero-shot and nearby scale-transfer evidence across load, hotspot, visibility, and scale variations.
\end{itemize}
